## Concept of the day — Unit 5 — The dollar cycle, segment 5 (capstone)

**Putting the dollar cycle together: a four-question checklist for any DXY move.** Segments 1 through 4 built this toolkit piece by piece: why a stronger dollar is itself a tightening event, not just a currency move (segment 1); why rate differentials pull capital toward the dollar in the first place — carry (segment 2); how that strength exports tightening abroad through dollar debt and dollar-priced commodities (segment 3); and how to read today's level against the dollar's own multi-decade cycle rather than just the past six months (segment 4). Put together, that's four questions for any DXY move. One: how big, and against which yardstick — the 120-day z-score for the last six months, or the all-time and 30-year percentiles for the full cycle (§4F)? Two: what's pulling it — a widening rate differential, or a safe-haven bid that has nothing to do with yield? Three: who feels it first — non-U.S. dollar borrowers paying more in local currency, commodity importers facing pricier dollar-denominated oil and copper, or U.S. multinationals translating foreign sales into fewer dollars? Four: does the rest of the data agree, or is the dollar moving alone? Today's own numbers run the checklist: DXY is unchanged from Friday at 102.21 (bond markets are shut for Columbus Day, but FX and stocks aren't) — still the 72nd percentile since 1971 and the 81st over the last 30 years. Strong by recent memory, firm but not extreme against the full cycle.
