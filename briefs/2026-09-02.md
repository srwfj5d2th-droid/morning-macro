## Concept of the day

**Unit 2 · Real vs. nominal decomposition · segment 3 of roughly 6 — The real yield has two pieces too: expected path and real term premium**

The last two segments split the nominal 10-year yield into a real yield (TIPS) and a breakeven (implied inflation). Today, one layer deeper: the real yield itself splits into the expected path of real short-term rates (roughly, how tight the Fed will keep policy after backing out inflation) and the real term premium (extra compensation for the risk of holding a 10-year bond instead of rolling over short-term ones). The real yield has risen for two straight sessions (2.42% Friday → 2.44% Monday) alongside rising fed-hike odds reported this morning (~68%, up from 40% a week ago) — that combination points toward the expected-path piece doing the work, which is exactly what should happen when the market moves the odds of an actual Fed decision. This system can't yet decompose real yields into path vs. premium numerically (that needs a term-structure model beyond what this system computes), so today's read is qualitative, not script-verified like the real/breakeven split — a limit of what this brief can currently prove, flagged as such.

**Client translation:** "Even the 'real' part of a bond yield has two pieces — how tight the Fed will actually be, and how much extra investors want for the uncertainty of a ten-year commitment — and right now the moves line up with the market believing the Fed, not just getting nervous about holding long bonds."
