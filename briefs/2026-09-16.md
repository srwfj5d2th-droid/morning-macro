## Concept of the day — Unit 3, Credit spreads, segment 5 of ~10

**Why flat credit spreads heading into a known event are informative, not just quiet.** A known, scheduled event (today's Fed decision) sits hours away, and credit spreads are essentially flat. That's not an absence of a data point — corporate bond investors can price a known event in advance, and if today's decision were likely to surprise the market in a way that raised real default or liquidity risk, credit would typically start drifting wider beforehand. It hasn't: HY OAS ticked up but stays well below its trailing average, IG OAS is flat. That's consistent with a decision the market believes is close to fully priced — a different, generally lower-risk situation than a coin-flip event credit hasn't yet reacted to.

**Client translation:** "Corporate bond markets aren't nervous heading into today's Fed decision — and that's useful information, because it means professional lenders think this is priced in and won't cause real financial stress, which is different from an unexpected shock."
