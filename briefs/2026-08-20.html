The story

The easy story this morning is "dovish Fed relief rally." The wire coverage says otherwise, and the distinction matters. The July minutes, released at 2pm, actually showed a split committee with several officials having favored a rate hike (CNBC, Newsquawk) — hawkish at the margin. What moved markets was the other 2pm-adjacent headline: Treasury announced it would at least double the size of its long-end bond buyback operations (Babypips). Yields dropped — 30Y −9bp to 5.19%, 10Y −6bp to 4.65% — the dollar sank 0.8% to 98.83, gold jumped 2.8% to 4,489, and stocks ground higher into the close. If you read only one line today: the long end was repriced by a change in supply mechanics, not by a change in the policy outlook. Relief by intervention is still relief — but it tells you the pain threshold of the debt manager, not the conviction of the market (this is Unit 4 territory, liquidity plumbing; the curriculum gets there in a few weeks).

Under the macro headline, Wednesday was a breadth day of historic shape: Moderna posted the largest one-day gain by any S&P 500 stock this century (+177% — Seeking Alpha, Motley Fool), health care diverged +3.3pp from the index, and the equal-weight S&P rose 1.04% against the cap-weighted 0.21% — pushing equal-weight to a +1.85σ reading on its own 120-day history, this morning's only z-flag and the chart of the day. Meanwhile the AI-hardware unwind did not get the memo: SMH fell another 1.55% (Seagate −7.9%) on a day the tape rallied. The market is broadening and rotating at the same time — which is what "risk-on/diverging" means on the masthead.

Test against the state file: credit stayed boring (HY OAS 2.75%, z −0.6; IG 0.82%) — consistent with rotation, inconsistent with stress. Breakevens barely moved (2.30%, z −0.4), confirming Wednesday's yield drop was a real-rate/premium move, not an inflation repricing — CL-0003 holds. The S&P closed 1.17% from its all-time closing high with the average stock outperforming; nothing in rates, credit, or FX contradicts the equity tape today. The open question is the one CL-0002 tracks: whether AI hardware is a rotation casualty or a message.

Concept of the day · Unit 1 · Yield curve mechanics · segment 2 of ~10

Path versus premium — and how to tell which one moved

Yesterday's segment said every long yield is a forecast plus a premium: the expected average of overnight rates (the path), plus extra compensation for holding long paper through uncertainty (the term premium). Wednesday handed us a clean dissection. If the 30Y had fallen 9bp because the path changed, you would expect a cause that changes the Fed outlook — but the minutes leaned the other way (several officials favored a hike), and the front end, which is nearly all path, did not move (2Y flat at 4.19%). Instead the trigger was Treasury doubling its long-end buybacks — a change in the supply of long paper the market must absorb. Less supply to warehouse means less compensation demanded for warehousing it: that is a term-premium move, almost surgically isolated. This is why the decomposition matters: a path-driven rally says "the economy or the Fed changed"; a premium-driven rally says "the plumbing changed." They feel identical in a bond fund's daily return and mean entirely different things for what comes next.

Client translation: "Long-term rates fell yesterday, but not because anyone thinks the Fed is cutting sooner — the Treasury said it would buy back more long bonds, so there's less for the market to hold. Same lower rate, very different reason — and the reason is what tells you if it lasts."
