## Concept of the day

**Unit 3 · Credit spreads · segment 3 of ~10 — A rate spike isn't a risk spike until credit says so: using OAS as the tie-breaker between a "clean" Fed move and something breaking**

Today's dashboard has two things happening at once that are easy to blur together: Treasury yields just posted this system's largest statistical flag ever, and stocks are still near all-time highs. The instinct is to treat "yields spiking" as inherently scary — it isn't, on its own, and credit spreads are how you tell the difference. When yields rise because the Fed is expected to raise rates on a *strong* economy (today's case), credit spreads usually stay calm, because a strong economy is good for a borrower's ability to repay — exactly what's showing up now: both HY and IG OAS are unflagged. Contrast a rate spike that happens because markets are pricing recession or default risk: there, credit spreads widen right alongside yields, often faster. The practical rule: don't read a Treasury-yield move as a risk signal by itself — check whether credit is confirming it. Today, it isn't, which is the specific, falsifiable reason this system's regime tag stays "tightening" and not "stress."

**Client translation:** "Rates jumping because the Fed might raise them isn't the same kind of scary as rates jumping because something's breaking — the tell is whether corporate borrowing costs are moving too, and right now they're not."
