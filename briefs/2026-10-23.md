## The story

**The AI-capex story moved from financing talk to the equipment makers themselves today.** Lam Research reported a record quarter — $8.22 billion in revenue and $2.20 adjusted EPS, both ahead of Street estimates (24/7 Wall St, Invezz, Benzinga) — and still fell 11.76% to $230.15, because its December-quarter revenue guide of $8.2–9.0 billion came in below the $8.53 billion analysts expected. The company's own CFO tied the shortfall to “near-term supply chain constraints,” not weaker customer orders (Benzinga); Lam simultaneously raised its 2026–2027 wafer-fab-equipment spending forecast, now expecting the industry to spend over $200 billion in 2027. Applied Materials and ASML fell the same session on the same read-through (24/7 Wall St), and the rest of the chip-equipment and AI-optical complex — KLA, Teradyne, Coherent, Lumentum, Ciena, Marvell, Micron, Western Digital and SanDisk among them — sold off together; this system found no distinct, same-day news for any of those names beyond the shared sector read, so that's reported as a pattern in Tier 3 (below), not individually attributed (§5).

**Credit gave a little back, but the tightening flag didn't break.** HY OAS (dated Wednesday Oct 21, the usual one-day lag) eased 6bp to 3.27% — still this system's largest flag for a seventh straight session, even as the z-score itself cooled slightly from Wednesday's reading. IG OAS, also dated Oct 21, ticked up 1bp to 0.88%, the second-largest flag. Two conditional claims resolved this morning on exactly this pattern (Claims, above): CL-0045, confirmed, found the equal-weight RSP beating the cap-weighted SPY for the full week; CL-0046, also confirmed, found the credit-spread widening that began two weeks ago held through every print in its test window. Today's own breadth was the starkest version of that pattern yet: the S&P 500 fell 1.21% and the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.60%, led by SMH's 5.99% drop — but the equal-weight RSP actually rose 0.73%, and the Russell 2000 inched up 0.11%. For the first time this run, the average didn't just fall by less than the index; it moved the opposite direction entirely (today's concept, below).

**Put together, today reads as sector-specific, not systemic.** The selling sat almost entirely in chip-equipment, AI-optical, and a handful of AI-power-adjacent names (Tier 3, below), while Consumer Staples (+3.31% vs. SPY), Utilities (+2.07%) and Health Care (+1.32%) all rallied — a rotation, not a retreat, by the same breadth test this curriculum has been building toward all month. Treasury yields easing across the curve on the same day stocks sold off looks like a flight to safety, not a credit event: the one series that would say otherwise, HY OAS, eased rather than widened today. Credit's seventh straight flagged session is the one piece of the day that still argues for caution rather than comfort.
