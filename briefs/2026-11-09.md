Friday's bounce is real; it just isn't yet confirmed.

## Concept of the day — Unit 8 — Cross-asset confirmation, segment 3

**When agreement doesn't last — reapplying the hierarchy the very next session.** Segment 2 showed the clean case: Thursday, stocks, credit, and bonds all moved together, and agreement across markets that don't have to agree was itself the strongest signal available. Friday hands the unit the next lesson, and it's an uncomfortable one: that agreement didn't survive one session. Stocks bounced; credit widened again; the dollar and the front end of the curve moved in the hawkish direction on a jobs report that argued for a less generous Fed, not a more generous one. Segment 1's hierarchy — when markets disagree, weight credit over equity sentiment — isn't a one-time tool you use once and retire. It has to be reapplied every single morning, because yesterday's agreement is not a standing fact about today's market; it's a reading that can and does expire. The discipline this teaches isn't “credit is always right” — it's that confirmation has to be checked fresh each day, the same way this brief reconciles its claims ledger fresh each morning rather than assuming yesterday's conclusion still holds.

**Client translation:** "Just because the market agreed with itself yesterday doesn't mean it still does today -- check the signal fresh every morning instead of assuming it's still true."

## Client lens

**“The market bounced back Friday, so are we past the worst of it?”** Stocks did bounce, but the part of the market that tends to be the more reliable warning sign — credit spreads — got wider, not narrower, the same day. That disagreement, not the headline index number, is the thing worth watching into this week.

**“I heard the jobs report was good — isn't that bullish?”** A strong jobs report is good news about the economy, but it's being read by markets as raising the odds the Fed holds rates higher for longer (or even hikes in December), which is why short-term bond yields and the dollar rose Friday even as stocks rallied. Good economic news and a friendlier Fed don't always arrive together.

**“What happened with Axon and Coherent?”** Two unrelated, company-specific stories: Axon missed earnings and cut guidance (down 18%); Coherent, which had actually beaten its own earnings guidance Wednesday night but sold off anyway, got an analyst price-target upgrade Friday and partly recovered (up 14%). Neither is a read on the broader market — both are useful reminders that a single day's stock move can be about one company's numbers, not the macro picture.
