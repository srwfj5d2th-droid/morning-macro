## The story

Reporting this morning centers on the rebound. CNBC and TheStreet report U.S. equities climbed Thursday as Treasury yields and oil retreated, clawing back Wednesday's post-Fed selloff — the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite both closed higher, led by "Magnificent Seven" names Nvidia and Amazon (both up over 2%) and Microsoft (+1.5%). The sharpest move was in chips: 24/7 Wall St. and TradingKey report Intel jumped roughly 8%, AMD 7%, and Micron over 5%, in what both outlets explicitly frame as a technical, positioning-driven squeeze — "explosive call [options] activity" in Intel and dip-buying in Micron and AMD forcing short sellers to cover, a third straight session of the rebound — rather than a fresh fundamental reassessment of the AI trade. **Does the state file confirm the press's framing?** On the rebound itself, cleanly: the S&P 500 rose 1.14% and the Nasdaq-100 1.69%, both real, broad moves. But the state file adds something the "market recovers" framing alone doesn't: credit's first same-day reaction to Wednesday's actual decision. High-yield and investment-grade spreads — both now dated Wednesday 09-16, the decision day itself — eased rather than widened, the opposite of what a "tightening financial conditions" reading of the hawkish dot plot would predict. That's a genuine complication of the loudest version of Wednesday's "hawkish surprise" story, not a confirmation of Thursday's "relief rally" framing either — credit was never rattled in the first place. On the chip rebound, the state file leans toward the technical read reporting gave it: SMH jumped 2.76%, narrowing CL-0015's gap to its tightest reading yet, but the front-end Treasury market told a matching story — the 2-year gave back 7bp of Wednesday's move, a partial fade of the very hawkish surprise chip traders were reportedly squeezing out of, not an independent AI-conviction signal. Both threads point the same direction: Thursday looks like digestion and positioning unwinding a sharp Wednesday move, not a new piece of information.

## Concept of the day — Unit 3, Credit spreads, segment 7 of ~10

**A cushion, not just calm — credit tightening on a hawkish surprise is its own signal, distinct from merely staying flat.** Segment 6 (yesterday) drew the line between credit staying flat ahead of a known event and credit's actual reaction once it lands. Today's fill answers that test, and more sharply than a simple pass: on the day the Fed delivered a hike plus a hawkish dot plot press called the real surprise, high-yield spreads didn't just hold steady — they eased 6bp, and investment-grade eased 2bp. Flat credit says lenders saw nothing new to reprice; tightening credit says something stronger — that on a day risk should have looked marginally worse, the market that prices exactly that risk decided borrowers looked marginally safer instead. One honest limit: OAS is a single-day, aggregate number, and one day of tightening is a data point, not a trend — which is what CL-0019 is built to test with the next fill. The lesson generalizes: credit's direction on the day of a hawkish surprise, not just its level beforehand, is itself evidence.

**Client translation:** "The Fed just signaled it's not done raising rates, but the part of the market that actually lends money to companies got a little more comfortable, not less — that's a genuinely reassuring signal, though one day of it isn't proof the coast is clear."
