## Concept of the day — Unit 5 — The dollar cycle, segment 1

**Why a stronger dollar tightens conditions everywhere, not just at the currency desk.** New unit today. The dollar (DXY, this system's own tracked index) isn't just a price quote between currencies — it's the unit a huge share of the world's debt and trade is priced in, so its level is itself a lever on financial conditions everywhere, not only in foreign-exchange markets. When the dollar strengthens, three things happen roughly at once: dollar-denominated debt held by non-U.S. borrowers (governments, companies, emerging-market issuers) gets more expensive to service in their own currency, even if the interest rate on that debt never moves; commodities priced in dollars (oil, copper, gold) get more expensive for the rest of the world, which can crimp global demand; and U.S. companies with large overseas revenue see those sales translate back into fewer dollars, a drag on reported earnings that has nothing to do with how the business actually performed. None of that requires the Fed to do anything — a stronger dollar is tightening that happens on its own, through trade and debt markets rather than a rate decision. Today's DXY actually eased a touch (−0.33%, Dashboard above) after days of climbing (still flagged, z +1.82) — today's small pullback doesn't undo that build-up, it just pauses it.

**Client translation:** "A stronger dollar is like a global interest-rate hike nobody voted on — it raises the real cost of debt and squeezes overseas profits even if the Fed never touches a rate."
