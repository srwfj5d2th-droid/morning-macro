## The story

The easy story this morning is "dovish Fed relief rally." The wire coverage says otherwise, and the distinction matters. The July minutes showed a split committee with several officials having favored a rate *hike* (CNBC, Newsquawk) — hawkish at the margin. What moved markets was the other headline: Treasury announced it would at least double its long-end bond buyback operations (Babypips). Yields dropped — 30Y −9bp to 5.19%, 10Y −6bp to 4.65% — the dollar sank 0.8% to 98.83, gold jumped 2.8% to 4,489, and stocks ground higher. The long end was repriced by a change in *supply mechanics*, not the *policy outlook*. Relief by intervention is still relief — but it tells you the pain threshold of the debt manager, not the conviction of the market.

Under the macro headline, Wednesday was a breadth day of historic shape: Moderna posted the largest one-day gain by any S&P 500 stock this century (+177%), health care diverged +3.3pp, and the equal-weight S&P rose 1.04% against the cap-weighted 0.21% — pushing equal-weight to +1.85σ on its 120-day history, this morning's only z-flag. ## Concept of the day — Unit 1 · segment 2

**Path versus premium — and how to tell which one moved.** Every long yield is a forecast plus a premium: the expected average of overnight rates (the *path*) plus compensation for holding long paper through uncertainty (the *term premium*). Wednesday was a clean dissection: if the 30Y had fallen 9bp on the *path*, you'd expect a Fed-outlook cause — but the minutes leaned hawkish and the 2Y (nearly all path) didn't move. The trigger was Treasury doubling long-end buybacks — a change in *supply*. Less long paper to warehouse, less compensation demanded: a term-premium move, surgically isolated. A path-driven rally says "the economy or the Fed changed"; a premium-driven rally says "the plumbing changed." Identical in a bond fund's daily return; entirely different for what comes next.

**Client translation:** "Long-term rates fell yesterday, but not because anyone thinks the Fed is cutting sooner — the Treasury said it would buy back more long bonds, so there's less for the market to hold. Same lower rate, very different reason — and the reason is what tells you if it lasts."
