## Concept of the day — Unit 1 · segment 3

**Naming the move: steepeners, flatteners, and what each one says.** Analysts compress every curve move into two words: direction (*bull* = yields down, *bear* = up) and slope (*steepener* / *flattener*). This week was a full specimen set. Monday: **bear steepener** — long yields up, front pinned, 2s10s to +52bp — the market demanding more term premium, the most uncomfortable move for equities. Wednesday: **bull flattener** from the long end — 30Y −9bp on buybacks, 2Y still. Thursday: the bear steepener returned in miniature. Each name is a sentence about *who moved*: when the front end drives, the market is re-voting on the Fed; when the long end drives — as it did all week — the vote is about premium and supply, and the Fed is a spectator. The 2Y's total range this week: zero. That is how you know which regime you're in.

**Client translation:** "All the drama in bonds this week happened in 20- and 30-year paper — short rates never moved. Markets aren't arguing about what the Fed does next; they're arguing about what it costs to lend the government money for decades."
