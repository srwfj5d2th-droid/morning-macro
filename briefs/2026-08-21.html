Concept of the day · Unit 1 · Yield curve mechanics · segment 3 of ~10

Naming the move: steepeners, flatteners, and what each one says

Analysts compress every curve move into a two-word name: which way yields went (bull = down, bear = up, from the bondholder's view) and what the slope did (steepener = long end moved more, flattener = front end moved more, or the long end moved toward the front). This week was a full specimen set. Monday: a bear steepener — long yields up, front end pinned, 2s10s widening to +52bp — the market demanding more term premium, the most uncomfortable move for equities. Wednesday: a bull flattener from the long end — 30Y −9bp on the buyback news while the 2Y sat still, slope back to +46bp. Thursday: the bear steepener returned in miniature (+4bp long, slope +50bp). Each name is a sentence about who moved: when the front end drives, the market is re-voting on the Fed; when the long end drives — as it did all week — the vote is about premium and supply, with the Fed a spectator. The 2Y's total range this week: zero. That is how you know which regime you are in.

Client translation: "All the drama in bonds this week happened in 20- and 30-year paper — short rates never moved. That means markets aren't arguing about what the Fed does next; they're arguing about what it costs to lend the government money for decades."
