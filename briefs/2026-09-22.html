Does the state file confirm the press's framing? Two threads corroborate it: credit spreads tightened again — HY OAS eased 2bp and IG OAS 1bp (both dated 09-18, Dashboard below), a third straight session of calm-to-tighter credit, not the complacent-then-cracks pattern a real risk buildup would eventually show (though one day never proves that either way — see today's Concept, below); and the long end of the Treasury curve eased rather than fought the rally, with the 10-year and 30-year each down 5bp on the day. The one piece of the tape still flagged and unmoved is mechanical, not a market signal: SOFR and the front end are still digesting last week's actual rate hike (CL-0020, Claims above), not expressing doubt about today's rally. One plausible reading, not asserted as fact: a single company's product-launch data point (an app's App Store rank) doing this much work across an entire sector in one session is unusual, and CL-0021 (Claims, above) is the falsifiable version of whether it holds.

Concept of the day · Unit 3 · Credit spreads · segment 9 of ~10

Tight spreads have a blind spot too — OAS as a mirror, not a crystal ball

Segments 3 and 4 built OAS into a tie-breaker: a scary rate spike or a violent single-day stock swing is only a real risk event if credit spreads confirm it by widening. That's a genuinely useful test, and this system has leaned on it for two weeks. But it has a mirror-image blind spot worth naming honestly. OAS measures what lenders are demanding right now, given current default and liquidity conditions — it's a photograph, not a forecast. Spreads can stay tight, or keep tightening, for a long stretch precisely during the period when risk is quietly building rather than showing up yet — the textbook real-world case is 2006-2007, when credit spreads sat near historic lows in the months just before a systemic crack. None of that is a claim about today: HY and IG OAS both eased again this morning, a third straight session of calm-to-tighter credit during the year's most powerful one-day equity rally (Dashboard, above), and one session of compression proves nothing either way about which kind of quiet this is. The discipline this system applies instead: treat sustained spread compression as informative about the present, not predictive about the future, and keep watching for the second and third data points the way segment 8 described — because the only honest answer to "is this calm or complacency" is that OAS alone can't tell you, and claiming otherwise would be exactly the kind of overconfident narration §4C exists to prevent.

Client translation: "Tight credit spreads tell you nothing's broken right now — they don't tell you nothing's building. I watch them because they're a good photograph of today, not because they're a forecast of next year, which is why I never let one calm reading do all the talking."
