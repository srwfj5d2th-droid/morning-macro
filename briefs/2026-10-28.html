The story

The Fed decides today, and credit didn't wait for the answer. Fed funds are expected to hold at 4.00% at 2pm ET, unchanged from last month's hike — the first since 2023 — with Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30pm the part markets will parse hardest (CNBC, Yahoo Finance). Going into the decision, IG OAS (dated Monday 10-26, the usual one-day lag) logged this run's largest flag for a second straight day — z +2.98 — with HY OAS close behind at +2.65 (Dashboard, below). Per §4E, this brief frames the decision rather than calling it in advance: a hawkish hold (language keeping the door open to another hike, continued emphasis on credit conditions) would read as consistent with CL-0052's policy-driven branch; a dovish hold (language leaning toward done-for-now) would read as more consistent with the technical/liquidity branch. Wednesday's statement and Thursday's reconciliation grade the claim — not a forecast made here.

Underneath the Fed story, earnings season split hard today. Boeing fell 10.69% after Tuesday's Q3 report showed its core loss narrowing but still missing the Street's view, with another cut to the 737 delivery forecast (RTTNews). Xylem fell 14.21% to a 52-week low after a Q3 revenue and profit miss and a cut to full-year guidance, with management citing slower conversion of orders to sales and utility customers delaying water-metering purchases (Benzinga, Seeking Alpha, Investing.com) — and the same morning, water-technology peer Pentair rose 12.80% on its own Q3 beat (SEC 8-K, RTTNews). Same sub-industry, same day, opposite directions: that pairing is itself the evidence this was a company-specific story on both sides, not a signal about water-infrastructure demand broadly. Meanwhile the chip and AI-hardware rally that started Monday on Anthropic's IPO filing ran a second day — Dell, Super Micro, Western Digital, Cadence, Coherent, ON Semiconductor and a wide swath of semiconductor names all gained 4-7.5% (Movers/Tier 3, below); this system found no distinct, dated catalyst specific to today beyond that continuation.

Put together, today is one macro test (the Fed) riding on top of a lot of idiosyncratic micro noise. None of the individual earnings moves says anything about the broader economy on its own — that's exactly what the Boeing/Xylem/Pentair pattern shows. The one item without a same-day explanation worth tracking formally is this morning's refiner selloff (Movers, below), logged today as a new conditional claim (CL-0053, Flags) rather than guessed at.

Reading key
bp (basis point) = 1/100th of a percent — "+4bp" on a yield means it rose 0.04%.  z(120d) = how unusual today's level is versus the past ~6 months of trading days; 0 is typical, and anything beyond ±1.5 gets a ⚑ flag — far enough outside its recent normal range to deserve attention.  %ile all/30y = where today's level ranks against this series' real history — the all-time percentile, then the trailing-30-year percentile, since pooling 60+ years can hide a regime shift (a few extreme years can make "normal" look lower or higher than it's felt in the modern era) — 50 is the median either way; † flags when the two readings disagree by 20 points or more, which is itself the finding.  HY / IG OAS = the extra yield investors demand to lend to junk-rated (HY) or investment-grade (IG) companies instead of the government; widening = worry, calm = confidence — credit is the market's smoke detector.  2s10s / 3m10s = long-term Treasury yield minus short-term; the slope of the yield curve.  Regime tags: calm · risk-on/confirming · risk-on/diverging · tightening · stress.
