Claims ledger — one confirms today, one expires mixed

CL-0073 (Thursday 11-19's broad four-way risk-off agreement marking a genuine extension of the selloff, not a one-day echo) — expires mixed/inconclusive. All three test sessions are in: Friday 11-20 closed at 6,910.39 (below the 7,175.83 confirm reference), but Monday 11-23 (7,237.17) and Tuesday 11-24 (7,201.09) both closed back above it — only 1 of 3 sessions below, short of the required “at least 2 of 3.” The index never reached the 7,369 refute level either (window high 7,237.17). Neither the four-way extension nor the one-day-echo read was confirmed; credit (HY/IG OAS) stayed flagged throughout, but price action didn't cooperate with either side of the test.

CL-0074 (Friday 11-20's AI/semiconductor-concentrated unwind reflecting a genuine, still-concentrated repricing rather than a broadening market-wide panic) — confirmed, inside the first session of its 5-day window. Today SMH fell 7.27% (460.18 to 426.75) while the S&P 500 fell only 0.50% — a 6.77-point divergence, well past the 4-point confirm bar — and both Health Care (XLV, +2.95%) and Consumer Staples (XLP, +1.66%) outperformed the S&P 500 the same session, meeting the defensive-sector leg too (Movers, below). The chip/AI-hardware complex took another hit today while staples and health care held up again — still concentrated, not broadening.

The story

The story this morning, per quantisnow's same-day recap (“Analog Devices falls 17.3%, dragging the Nasdaq 100 down while equal-weight stocks rise”) and cnttrading's market letter (“Chip stocks give back the Huang rally as money rotates to retail”), is a reversal of Monday's Jensen-Huang-reinstatement rally. Analog Devices fell 17.37% to $312.95 even after beating Wall Street's revenue and profit estimates (Investing.com, Benzinga — adjusted EPS $4.06 vs. roughly $3.89 consensus, revenue $4.37B vs. roughly $4.31–4.32B consensus), with neither outlet tying the drop to anything specific in the print itself. Hewlett Packard Enterprise fell 16.31% to $40.19 despite beating its own earnings guidance and raising fiscal 2027 targets substantially (company release, The Motley Fool's earnings-call transcript, CryptoBriefing), while flagging that operating margin will moderate and that memory-component supply may stay tight into late calendar 2028. Micron fell 11.01% on a sharply cut BNP Paribas price target, $775 from $1,700 (247wallst), tied to memory-pricing worries ahead of Dell's own earnings; Dell itself fell 11.83% to $295.28 in the regular session, then reported record third-quarter results and a $25 billion buyback after the bell (Yahoo Finance, TradingKey) — that after-hours move won't show up in this system's numbers until tomorrow. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.48% to 23,733.54 and the S&P 500 fell a smaller 0.50% to 7,201.09, giving back a slice of Monday's 4.73% and 6.51% gains.

This system's own numbers complicate the “market gave back the rally” read in one specific way: the giveback was narrow, not broad. The equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP) actually rose 0.54% while the cap-weighted SPY fell 0.49% — a mirror image of Monday's gap, now running the other direction — and the Russell 2000 rose 0.57%. Credit spreads, which should widen first on a genuine risk-off day (Unit 3, Concept below), instead eased: HY OAS fell 13bp and IG OAS fell 1bp on their Monday-dated prints, both still flagged on the elevated 120-day trend but moving the calmer direction on a day the chip complex fell double digits. That combination — a severe, concentrated equity move with credit easing and breadth improving — is the live case for today's regime tag and for marking CL-0074 confirmed rather than broadening (Claims, above). Two Tier 1 readings, the 30-year Treasury and the 10-year real yield (the latter's own 23-year record reading milder once cross-checked against the Cleveland Fed's 1982-on model, History below), are still echoing levels last touched, before this run, in 2007 and 2008 respectively — worth holding in mind as today's real test arrives: Core PCE and the preliminary Q3 GDP print, both due 8:30am ET, are the first hard macro data this week that isn't one company's earnings call.

Treasury yields were almost silent today while stocks did the moving. The 3-month bill eased 1bp to 4.36% (z +1.82, flagged — higher than all but a handful of days in the past six months; 54th percentile since the early 1980s, 73rd of the last 30 years). The 2-year held flat at 4.63% (z +0.73, below the flag line; 50th percentile over the ~50-year record but the 79th of the last 30 years, † a 29-point gap). The 10-year eased 1bp to 4.91% (z +0.42; 45th/82nd %ile, †). The 30-year ticked up 1bp to 5.30% (z +0.31; 46th/81st %ile, † — today's lead History-check fact, below: before this run, this level was last touched, briefly, in June 2007). The curve barely budged (2s10s −1bp to 28bp, z −1.30; 3m10y unchanged at 55bp, z −1.97, flagged). Why this matters: a bond is a loan, and when nearly the whole curve sits still on a day a whole sector of stocks falls double digits, that's a sign today's move is a stock-specific repricing, not a change in the growth-and-rates outlook that mortgage rates and bond funds trade on.

Credit and the dollar both moved the calmer direction today, which is the real tell. HY OAS — the extra yield investors demand to lend to junk-rated companies over safe Treasurys — eased 13bp to 3.63% on its Monday-dated print (z +2.57, flagged; 94.5th percentile of its own ~3-year record, marked * since this live series has no multi-decade history, §4F). IG OAS, the investment-grade equivalent, eased 1bp to 0.97% (also Monday-dated; z +2.76, flagged; 84.9th percentile of its own ~3-year record, *). A 40-year proxy for investment-grade spreads (Moody's Baa yield minus the 10-year Treasury, used only for direction and timing, never magnitude) sits at just the 13.7th percentile all-time and 10.2nd of the last 30 years — genuinely tight, which backs up today's ease rather than reading it as a quirk of a short series. The dollar (DXY) eased 0.49% to 103.81 but stayed flagged (z +2.25; 75th percentile over the ~55-year dollar record and the 84th of the last 30 years, †). Gold fell 2.45% to $3,561.90 (z −3.05, flagged) and copper fell 4.70% to $5.36/lb (z −4.32, this run's largest |z| of any series today, Chart of the day below), both extending their multi-week decline. Why this matters: when the two series that usually widen first on real stress (HY and IG OAS) instead ease on a day a whole sector falls double digits, that's informative on its own — evidence the selloff is contained to one part of the market, not spreading into the broader cost of borrowing that shows up in CDs, bond funds, and corporate loans.

Concept of the day · Unit 3 — Credit spreads, segment 4 reinforcement (R)

Dispersion isn't a credit signal until OAS says so — today's chip selloff, by the numbers

Segment 4 taught that a violent single-day move in individual stocks or a sector isn't automatically a credit event — check whether OAS, the extra yield lenders demand on top of Treasurys, actually moved before reading a big price swing as rising systemic risk. Today is a clean live case. The chip and AI-hardware complex fell hard: SMH −7.27%, Analog Devices −17.37%, Hewlett Packard Enterprise −16.31%, Micron −11.01%. If that were a genuine risk event, HY and IG OAS should have widened right alongside it. Instead both eased — HY OAS down 13bp, IG OAS down 1bp — on their Monday-dated prints.

Why this matters: dispersion (some stocks or sectors moving a lot) and systemic risk (the market's lenders getting nervous about everyone) are two different things, and only one of them should worry a diversified account. Today's test says this is dispersion, not a risk event — useful to know before assuming a scary headline about chip stocks means trouble for a client's broader bond or balanced fund.

Client translation: A rough day for chip stocks isn't automatically a rough day for the credit markets that set mortgage, CD, and bond-fund rates — today, the two didn't move together, and that's informative on its own.

Reading key
bp (basis point) = 1/100th of a percent — "+4bp" on a yield means it rose 0.04%.  z(120d) = how unusual today's level is versus the past ~6 months of trading days; 0 is typical, and anything beyond ±1.5 gets a ⚑ flag — far enough outside its recent normal range to deserve attention.  %ile all/30y = where today's level ranks against this series' real history — the all-time percentile, then the trailing-30-year percentile, since pooling 60+ years can hide a regime shift (a few extreme years can make "normal" look lower or higher than it's felt in the modern era) — 50 is the median either way; † flags when the two readings disagree by 20 points or more, which is itself the finding.  HY / IG OAS = the extra yield investors demand to lend to junk-rated (HY) or investment-grade (IG) companies instead of the government; widening = worry, calm = confidence — credit is the market's smoke detector.  2s10s / 3m10s = long-term Treasury yield minus short-term; the slope of the yield curve.  Regime tags: calm · risk-on/confirming · risk-on/diverging · tightening · stress.
