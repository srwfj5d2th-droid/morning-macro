## Concept of the day

**Unit 3 · Credit spreads · segment 2 of ~10 — Default premium vs. liquidity premium: the two things OAS pays you for, and why one company's story doesn't move the aggregate**

Yesterday introduced OAS as one number; it's really two worries bundled together. The **default premium** pays for the chance the borrower doesn't pay you back. The **liquidity premium** pays for a bond being hard to sell quickly, regardless of creditworthiness. HY OAS and IG OAS are each an average across hundreds of bonds — a broad temperature reading, not one borrower's chart. Oracle is a clean live example: its own credit story clearly deteriorated this week (a downgrade to one notch above junk, tens of billions in new debt, years of negative free cash flow) — exactly the kind of thing that should widen an individual bond's default premium. But the aggregate IG OAS this system tracks didn't move at all. One borrower's story, however large, doesn't move a diversified average unless the market starts pricing it as the leading edge of something broader — watching whether that changes is exactly what this system's OAS series is built to catch.

**Client translation:** "Oracle's own borrowing costs are a real story this week — a credit downgrade, tens of billions in new debt to fund AI data centers — but the broad measure of what companies overall pay to borrow hasn't budged. That's the difference between one company's story and a market-wide one, and right now, this is still just one company's."
