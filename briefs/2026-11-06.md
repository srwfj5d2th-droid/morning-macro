## Concept of the day — Unit 8 — Cross-asset confirmation, segment 2

**Agreement is the strongest signal — when every asset class moves the same way, there's no tie to break.** Segment 1 built a hierarchy for when asset classes disagree: weight credit over equity sentiment, and a single name over neither. Today hands the unit the opposite case, which matters just as much. The S&P 500 fell 4.36%, credit (HY OAS and IG OAS, Dashboard above) stayed in its own widened, flagged range rather than snapping back, and Treasury yields eased in a flight-to-quality move — three different corners of the market, three different kinds of participants (stock traders, bond buyers, and credit lenders), all moving the same direction the same day. When that happens, segment 1's hierarchy doesn't need to break a tie, because there isn't one: agreement across asset classes that don't have to agree is itself stronger evidence than any single market's move alone. The one asset that didn't confirm — the dollar, essentially flat at +0.04% — is worth naming precisely because it's the exception: a real signal doesn't require every market to move, but it's worth tracking whether the dollar's lack of reaction holds up or catches down over the next few sessions.

**Client translation:** "When stocks, bonds, and the credit market all move the same direction on the same day, that's a stronger signal than any one of them moving alone -- it's the market equivalent of three different witnesses telling the same story."
