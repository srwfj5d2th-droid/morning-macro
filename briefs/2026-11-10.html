The story

Monday's own coverage (Yahoo Finance, FX Leaders, BBN Times) centered on chips: AMD jumped 13.26% to $508.58 on an Oppenheimer upgrade to Outperform (new $600 price target) and news that Google Cloud selected AMD EPYC processors for its new TPU systems. The move pulled the sector with it — Broadcom rose about 9%, the PHLX/iShares semiconductor complex rose close to 7% — and the Nasdaq Composite closed at 25,253.14, up 673.71 points (2.74%); the S&P 500 added 2.35% to 7,549.94. Not every chip name joined: Nvidia fell 4.27% to $178.67 after its board opened an independent investigation Saturday into CEO Jensen Huang, following reports he was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, alongside his own disclosure of an affair (Coinpaper, Devdiscourse) — a company-specific governance story, not a read on AI demand, and one investor (Michael Burry, per Futunn) separately cautioned that the Nvidia-news-driven bounce in the rest of the group may not mark a new leg up.

This system's own pull backs the equity numbers and adds the piece Monday's headlines treated as separate from Friday's story. Credit didn't reverse Friday's widening, but it also didn't widen further: HY OAS (the extra yield lenders demand to hold junk-rated debt, still dated Friday 11-06 on its usual one-day lag) eased 6bp to 3.42% (z +2.46, still flagged, down from Friday's +2.83); IG OAS, the investment-grade equivalent, held flat at 0.93% (z +2.68, still flagged, essentially unchanged from Friday's +2.78). That is a real distinction worth holding onto: credit stopped getting worse, which is not the same thing as credit agreeing with Monday's rally — today's concept, below. Short rates, meanwhile, inched higher into tomorrow's CPI print: the 3-month bill rose 5bp to 4.24% (z +1.51, newly flagged — the one new Tier 1 flag today) and the 2-year rose 5bp to 4.56%, already above Friday's 4.51% confirm line for the live CL-0065 test. The dollar gave back some of Friday's hawkish-repricing gain, falling 0.64% to 101.65 (z +1.02, no longer flagged).

Two of the day's three qualifying movers were earnings- or analyst-driven, the third a governance story layered on top of the sector move: NRG Energy jumped 10.32% on a genuine Q3 beat and a $4bn asset-sale announcement, Nvidia fell on the board-investigation news even as its chip peers rallied, and AMD's gain traced to two dated, named catalysts rather than sector drift (Movers, below).

The front end of the curve is the part moving, and it's moving ahead of tomorrow's inflation print. The 3-month bill — the rate most directly set by where investors expect the Fed's policy rate to sit over the next few months — rose 5bp to 4.24% (z +1.51, newly flagged; 52nd percentile since 1977 but the 71st of the last 30 years, no real gap between the two). The 2-year, the part of the curve that functions as a running poll of Fed expectations, rose 5bp to 4.56% (z +0.69, not flagged; 49th percentile since 1977 but the 78th of the last 30 years, † — cite both, the two views disagree by 29 points). The 10-year rose 2bp to 4.88% (z +0.46; 45th/81st, †) and the 30-year was unchanged at 5.26% (z +0.24; 46th/81st, † — today's lead History-check fact, below, where both percentiles matter even more). Why this matters: when the short end moves first and the long end barely moves, the bond market is repricing how many more times (or how soon) the Fed acts, not the growth or inflation outlook for the next decade — that front-end move is closer to what can shift a new CD rate or a money-market yield than a 30-year mortgage quote.

The curve itself narrowed a touch on that front-end move: 2s10s fell 3bp to 32bp (flat over the past week), 3m10y fell 3bp to 64bp (6bp narrower over the week) — both comfortably positive, nowhere near the inversions on this morning's longer-run watch-list (History, below).

Credit stopped widening Friday, which is a different thing from credit agreeing with Monday's rally. HY OAS (dated Friday 11-06, the usual one-day lag) eased 6bp to 3.42% (z +2.46, still flagged; 85th percentile of its own ~3-year record, §4F, marked *, since this system's live HY-OAS source only runs that far back). IG OAS (also dated 11-06) held flat at 0.93% (z +2.68, still flagged; 74th percentile of its own ~3-year record, also *). SOFR, the overnight bank-to-bank borrowing rate (also dated 11-06), eased 3bp to 3.89% (z +1.44, no longer flagged, down from Friday's +1.71; 45th percentile since 1954 but the 67th of the last 30 years, †). Why this matters: a spread that stops widening is lenders no longer demanding more compensation for risk day over day; it isn't the same as lenders deciding the risk has actually eased, which is what a drop back below the |z| 1.5 flag would show.

The dollar (DXY) gave back some of Friday's hawkish-repricing gain, falling 0.64% to 101.65 (no longer flagged, z +1.02; 70th/79th percentile, no real gap). Fed balance-sheet and TGA readings are still last Wednesday's H.4.1/DTS release, carried forward unchanged — both are structurally modern-only instruments (there's no pre-2008 "normal" to compare against, §4F, so none is offered). ON RRP, the nearly drained overnight-cash shock absorber, ticked up to $0.25B (z −0.50, not flagged; also modern-only, no long-run comparison). Why this matters: a dollar that eases on a strong-stocks day is one less headwind for anyone holding foreign assets or dollar-priced commodities, though it's a small move against Friday's bigger one.

Concept of the day · Unit 8 — Cross-asset confirmation, segment 4

Stabilizing vs. confirming — why credit easing from "worse" to "flat" isn't the same as credit agreeing with the rally

Segment 3 showed Friday's disagreement hadn't gone away by Monday morning — stocks had bounced, credit had widened anyway. Monday adds a sharper distinction inside that same disagreement. HY OAS didn't widen again Friday; it actually eased 6bp. IG OAS didn't widen either; it just sat flat. On the surface, that looks like credit backing off its objection to the rally. It isn't, and the test for the difference is simple: did the reading cross back below the |z| 1.5 flag line, or did it just stop climbing? Both HY OAS (z +2.46) and IG OAS (z +2.68) are still flagged — still well outside their normal range, just no longer actively getting worse. That's the market-equivalent of a fever that stopped rising but hasn't broken. A client watching only the index number would see two up days and a cooling-off in the scary headlines and reasonably ask whether the all-clear has sounded; the honest answer this morning is "not yet, and here's the specific number that would tell us" — a drop back under |z| 1.5 on both series, not simply a quieter day. The discipline Unit 8 keeps teaching is the same one, applied one notch more precisely each day: don't let a signal's direction get mistaken for its level.

Client translation: Credit spreads easing off their worst reading isn't the same as credit giving the rally a thumbs-up — ask whether the number actually got back to normal, not just whether it stopped getting worse.

Reading key
bp (basis point) = 1/100th of a percent — "+4bp" on a yield means it rose 0.04%.  z(120d) = how unusual today's level is versus the past ~6 months of trading days; 0 is typical, and anything beyond ±1.5 gets a ⚑ flag — far enough outside its recent normal range to deserve attention.  %ile all/30y = where today's level ranks against this series' real history — the all-time percentile, then the trailing-30-year percentile, since pooling 60+ years can hide a regime shift (a few extreme years can make "normal" look lower or higher than it's felt in the modern era) — 50 is the median either way; † flags when the two readings disagree by 20 points or more, which is itself the finding.  HY / IG OAS = the extra yield investors demand to lend to junk-rated (HY) or investment-grade (IG) companies instead of the government; widening = worry, calm = confidence — credit is the market's smoke detector.  2s10s / 3m10s = long-term Treasury yield minus short-term; the slope of the yield curve.  Regime tags: calm · risk-on/confirming · risk-on/diverging · tightening · stress.
