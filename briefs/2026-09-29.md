## Concept of the day — Unit 4, Liquidity plumbing, segment 3

**The reserves identity — why a Treasury refunding shows up in liquidity before it shows up anywhere else.** Segments 1 and 2 named the three pieces of the Fed's plumbing: bank reserves, the Treasury General Account (TGA, the government's checking account at the Fed), and the overnight reverse repo facility (ON RRP, a parking spot for spare cash). They fit one identity: reserves ≈ Fed balance sheet minus TGA minus ON RRP. Running today's own numbers (all thin-history, directional-only per §4): $6,747,704M balance sheet, minus a $977,084M TGA, minus a now-tiny $851M ON RRP, leaves roughly $5.77 trillion in reserves — this system's own back-of-envelope from Thursday's H.4.1 release, not a precise real-time figure. The mechanism: when the Treasury issues a lot of debt at once — like the $70B five-year note auction behind CL-0022 — the cash it raises first lands in the TGA, which is the same as pulling that cash out of banking-system reserves. With ON RRP down to a sliver, there's very little cushion left to absorb a build that size. If reserves actually got scarce, SOFR creeping toward or through the top of the Fed's target range with no Fed hike behind it is where it would show up first. Today's SOFR reading is still the calm story CL-0020 already resolved; the identity above is the tool for recognizing the moment that stops being true.

**Client translation:** "The government's own checking account has grown by roughly $140 billion this month, and the spare-cash cushion that used to absorb swings like that is nearly empty — so if short-term borrowing rates wobble around the next big bout of Treasury issuance, that's the plumbing showing its limits, not the Fed changing policy."
