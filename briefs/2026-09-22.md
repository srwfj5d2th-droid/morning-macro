## Concept of the day — Unit 3, Credit spreads, segment 9 of ~10

**Tight spreads have a blind spot too — OAS as a mirror, not a crystal ball.** Segments 3 and 4 built OAS into a tie-breaker: a scary rate spike or violent single-day stock swing is only a real risk event if credit spreads confirm it by widening. That's genuinely useful, and this system has leaned on it for two weeks. But it has a mirror-image blind spot worth naming honestly. OAS measures what lenders are demanding *right now*, given *current* default and liquidity conditions — a photograph, not a forecast. Spreads can stay tight, or keep tightening, for a long stretch precisely during the period when risk is quietly building rather than showing up yet — the textbook real-world case is 2006–2007, when credit spreads sat near historic lows in the months just before a systemic crack. None of that is a claim about today: HY and IG OAS both eased again this morning, a third straight session of calm-to-tighter credit during the year's most powerful one-day equity rally, and one session of compression proves nothing either way about which kind of quiet this is. The discipline this system applies instead: treat sustained spread compression as informative about the present, not predictive about the future, and keep watching for the second and third data points — because the only honest answer to "is this calm or complacency" is that OAS alone can't tell you.

**Client translation:** "Tight credit spreads tell you nothing's broken right now — they don't tell you nothing's building. I watch them because they're a good photograph of today, not because they're a forecast of next year, which is why I never let one calm reading do all the talking."
