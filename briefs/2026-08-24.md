## Concept of the day — Unit 1 · segment 4

**When the front end moves, the vote is about the Fed.** All week the diagnostic was: the 2-year didn't move, so the drama wasn't about the Fed. Friday that changed. A 2-year note's return is dominated by the Fed's path over 24 months — little room for anything else — so it's effectively a live poll of Fed expectations. A 5bp jump after a 52-month-high survey = "odds of the Fed staying tighter just rose." Long bonds carry that same expectation *plus* the time premium (segment 2) — which is why hot data moves the whole curve while a supply announcement moves only the long end. Friday was the week's first genuine *path* repricing, and it reframes Jackson Hole: the market already moved its forecast; the Chair's speech validates it or fights it — and either answer moves the 2-year first.

**Client translation:** "Watch the 2-year Treasury when big news hits: it's the market's live forecast of what the Fed does next. It sat dead still through last week's drama — until Friday's strong economic report nudged it up. That was the market quietly saying rate cuts just got a little less likely."
