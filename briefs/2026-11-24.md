**A rally can be just as narrow as a selloff — today's bounce, by the numbers.** Segment 4 taught that the same breadth tool used to spot a concentrated selloff works just as well on a green day: compare the cap-weighted index to the equal-weight average. If the cap-weighted index rises a lot more than the average, the gains sat in a handful of giant stocks, not across the market. Today is close to a textbook case. SPY rose 4.71% while the equal-weight RSP rose only 2.59% — a 2.1-point gap. The Russell 2000, a pure small-cap gauge, rose 3.40%, in between the two. All three numbers are positive, but the gap says this bounce wasn't evenly shared: a cluster of AI-linked mega-caps — Nvidia foremost — did most of the lifting.

Why this matters: a narrow rally can fade faster than a broad one, because it depends on a smaller number of stocks continuing to lead. It doesn't mean today's move was fake — real money moved real prices — it means the bounce is still concentrated, the same way October's selloff was.
