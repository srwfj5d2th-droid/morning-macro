The story

Monday was this run's biggest rally yet. The S&P 500 rose 4.73% to 7,237.17, the Nasdaq Composite rose 6.51% to 24,089.56, and the Russell 2000 rose 3.40% to 2,669.66 — all after Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO (Form 8-K, 2026-11-23), eleven days after the board had accepted his resignation and said it had no plans to bring him back (South China Morning Post). Nvidia itself rose 31.48% to $165.05, adding back nearly $1 trillion in market value in a single session (Fool.com, Yahoo Finance), and the rally spread across the whole chip/AI-hardware complex — Dell +20.46%, Micron +19.04%, Marvell +18.37%, and well over a hundred other names (Movers, below). Technology (XLK, +8.97%) was the day's largest sector divergence from the market (SPY, +4.71%), by 4.26 points; Energy (XLE, −1.06%) and Consumer Staples (XLP, −0.09%) were the only two sector ETFs to fall. The move pulled the S&P 500 back to 7.44% below its October 6 record — out of the confirmed-correction territory (10%+ off the high) this brief noted Friday's 11.62% reading had reached.

This system's own pull confirms the headline but adds two wrinkles before calling this a clean reversal. First, the rally was narrow, not broad: SPY rose 4.71% while the equal-weight RSP rose only 2.59%, a 2.1-point gap (breadth, Concept below) — most of today's gain sat in a cluster of AI-linked mega-caps, not across the average stock, the same concentration this system found in October's selloff, just running the other way. Second, credit hasn't voted yet: HY and IG OAS are both still dated Friday 11-20 (the usual one-session publication lag) at 3.76% and 0.98%, both still flagged near their widest levels this run has seen — not because credit disagrees with today's rally, but because today's own print won't arrive until tomorrow. The dollar, the fourth hierarchy vote, kept climbing anyway (DXY +0.49% to 104.32, still flagged, CL-0072 confirmed above) — a reminder that not every part of the picture joined today's rally. Treasury yields, for their part, barely moved. The 10-year held at 4.92%. The 30-year eased 2bp to 5.29%, a level it last touched, before this run, briefly, in June 2007 (History, below). Rates simply sat out today's equity story. One sharp divergence inside the rally itself: CrowdStrike fell 8.92% the same morning it reported a record quarter and announced an all-stock deal to acquire rival Zscaler (Movers, below) — idiosyncratic to that one deal, not part of the broader AI-hardware move.

Concept of the day · Unit 6 — Positioning & sentiment vs. fundamentals, segment 4 reinforcement (R)

A rally can be just as narrow as a selloff — today's bounce, by the numbers

Segment 4 taught that the same breadth tool used to spot a concentrated selloff works just as well on a green day: compare the cap-weighted index to the equal-weight average. If the cap-weighted index rises a lot more than the average, the gains sat in a handful of giant stocks, not across the market. Today is close to a textbook case. SPY rose 4.71% while the equal-weight RSP rose only 2.59% — a 2.1-point gap. The Russell 2000, a pure small-cap gauge, rose 3.40%, in between the two. All three numbers are positive, but the gap says this bounce wasn't evenly shared: a cluster of AI-linked mega-caps — Nvidia foremost — did most of the lifting.

Why this matters: a narrow rally can fade faster than a broad one, because it depends on a smaller number of stocks continuing to lead. It doesn't mean today's move was fake — real money moved real prices — it means the bounce is still concentrated, the same way October's selloff was.

Client translation: Today's big rally was real, but it was driven mostly by a handful of giant tech stocks bouncing back — not by the whole market joining in equally, which is worth knowing before assuming every account recovered the same amount today.

Is the AI selloff over?” Nvidia's board brought Jensen Huang back as CEO, and the whole chip/AI-hardware group rallied hard with it — the S&P 500 is back out of its correction (7.44% off its October record, versus 11.62% on Friday). But credit spreads (HY and IG OAS) are still showing Friday's stressed reading because today's own number hasn't arrived yet — worth saying “we'll know more once credit weighs in” rather than calling this settled.

“Was today's rally broad, or just a few big stocks?” Mostly a few big stocks. The S&P 500 ETF (SPY) rose 4.71% while the equal-weight average (RSP) rose only 2.59% — a real but concentrated bounce, led by AI-linked names, the same pattern (in reverse) that drove October's selloff (Concept, above).

“What happened with CrowdStrike and Zscaler?” CrowdStrike agreed to buy security rival Zscaler in an all-stock deal the same morning it reported a record quarter, and its own stock fell nearly 9% even as the rest of the market rallied — a reminder that one company's acquisition can move its stock in the opposite direction from everything else, for reasons specific to that one deal.

Reading key
bp (basis point) = 1/100th of a percent — "+4bp" on a yield means it rose 0.04%.  z(120d) = how unusual today's level is versus the past ~6 months of trading days; 0 is typical, and anything beyond ±1.5 gets a ⚑ flag — far enough outside its recent normal range to deserve attention.  %ile all/30y = where today's level ranks against this series' real history — the all-time percentile, then the trailing-30-year percentile, since pooling 60+ years can hide a regime shift (a few extreme years can make "normal" look lower or higher than it's felt in the modern era) — 50 is the median either way; † flags when the two readings disagree by 20 points or more, which is itself the finding.  HY / IG OAS = the extra yield investors demand to lend to junk-rated (HY) or investment-grade (IG) companies instead of the government; widening = worry, calm = confidence — credit is the market's smoke detector.  2s10s / 3m10s = long-term Treasury yield minus short-term; the slope of the yield curve.  Regime tags: calm · risk-on/confirming · risk-on/diverging · tightening · stress.
