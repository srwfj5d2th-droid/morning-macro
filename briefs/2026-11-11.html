The story

Today's coverage (Yahoo Finance, CNBC, Kiplinger, Investing.com) centers on one number: October Core CPI rose 0.3% month-over-month, above the 0.2% consensus, with headline CPI up 0.4% against a 0.3% forecast — the third straight month core inflation has run at 0.3% (Investing.com attributed some of the heat to high energy costs and AI-buildout-related pricing). Treasury yields jumped on the print, led by the front end: the 2-year rose 17bp to 4.73%. Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he's “leaning toward” a December hike and that a 50-basis-point move “should be considered” if inflation keeps running hot (CNBC); CME FedWatch odds for a December hike jumped to roughly 88–90% (Investing.com), up from about 72% a week ago. Stocks opened lower and stayed there, with the Nasdaq Composite down about 0.8% at the open as Nvidia weighed on tech (Yahoo Finance).

This system's own pull backs the yield move and adds two pieces this morning's headlines didn't dwell on. First, credit kept improving rather than confirming the rate scare: HY OAS (the extra yield lenders demand to hold junk-rated debt, dated Monday 11-09 on its usual one-day lag) eased another 8bp to 3.34% (z +2.04, still flagged but down from Monday's +2.46), and IG OAS eased 2bp to 0.91% (z +2.19, down from +2.68) — the second straight session of easing, the live CL-0066 test (Flags, below). Second, the equity reaction was narrow, not broad: the S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,520.93 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.74%, but the equal-weight S&P (RSP) barely moved, down just 0.03% — today's concept, below. The damage concentrated in chips: the semiconductor ETF SMH fell another 0.78% to a fresh multi-week low (z −2.48, this run's largest single-series flag, Chart of the day), with Nvidia's 5.47% drop doing most of the work. The dollar firmed on the hawkish repricing, DXY up 0.57% to 102.23 (z +1.53, newly re-flagged).

Two of today's three qualifying movers were earnings- or guidance-driven and moved against the broader tape's weakness; the third, Nvidia, sits at the center of today's one concentrated pullback (Movers, below).

The 10-year rose 10bp to 4.98% (z +0.83; 46th/83rd, †) and the 30-year rose 9bp to 5.35% (z +0.69; 47th/82nd, † — today's lead History-check fact, below, where that gap matters even more). Why this matters: when the front end jumps on a data print like this, it's repricing how many more times (and how soon) the Fed hikes, not the growth or inflation outlook for the next decade — closer to what moves a new CD rate or a variable-rate loan than a 30-year mortgage quote, though today's move was broad enough up the curve to touch mortgage quotes too.

The curve itself flattened on that front-end-led move: 2s10s fell 7bp to 25bp (28th/29th percentile, no real gap, nowhere near inversion), while 3m10y actually widened 4bp to 68bp — the belly of the curve, not the very front, absorbed most of today's move.

Concept of the day · Unit 8 — Cross-asset confirmation, segment 5

When the hierarchy splits three ways, not two — rates, credit, and breadth can each be right about a different question

Segments 1-4 built the hierarchy habit on a two-way split: rates against credit, or stocks against credit. Today splits three ways, and each piece is actually answering a different question. Rates and the dollar answered the policy question, cleanly: a hot CPI print pushed the 2-year yield up 17bp and December hike odds to roughly 90% — a real, data-confirmed repricing (CL-0065), not noise. Credit answered the risk question, and gave the opposite-sounding signal: HY and IG OAS both kept easing today (z +2.04 and +2.19, down from +2.46 and +2.68), a second straight session of improvement, not deterioration. Equity breadth answered a third question entirely — how widely is this being felt — and the answer is narrow: the S&P 500 fell 0.38% but the equal-weight average barely moved, down just 0.03%, meaning the selling sat almost entirely in one sector (chips, Tier 3 above) rather than across the market. None of these three actually contradicts the others once you ask what each one is measuring: more Fed hikes are coming (rates), lenders aren't newly worried about defaults (credit), and today's selling was concentrated, not systemic (breadth). The hierarchy habit isn't about picking a winner among three readings — it's matching each one to the question it's actually equipped to answer.

Client translation: Rates moved today because the Fed is more likely to hike again in December — that's a different thing from markets getting nervous about defaults or a broad selloff, and today's data shows neither of those happening.

“Is the Fed raising rates again?” Today's hotter-than-expected inflation report pushed the market's odds of a December hike to around 90%, from about 72% a week ago. If that holds, it argues for things tied to short rates — new CD yields, money-market rates, credit-card APRs — staying higher for longer, even if a 30-year mortgage quote (tied more to the long end) doesn't move as much.

“Why did Nvidia drop again?” Today's drop was partly the broader market's reaction to the inflation report (tech led the selling) and partly the still-unresolved board investigation into the CEO's conduct — two separate things stacking on the same stock. Neither one is new information about whether companies are still buying AI chips.

“The market barely moved today, right?” The headline S&P 500 number (down 0.38%) undersells how narrow today's decline was — the average stock, measured without letting the giant tech names dominate, was basically flat. Two of today's three biggest movers (Charter, Take-Two) were actually up sharply on their own company news. Worth remembering before assuming a down headline day means a bad day for every account.

Reading key
bp (basis point) = 1/100th of a percent — "+4bp" on a yield means it rose 0.04%.  z(120d) = how unusual today's level is versus the past ~6 months of trading days; 0 is typical, and anything beyond ±1.5 gets a ⚑ flag — far enough outside its recent normal range to deserve attention.  %ile all/30y = where today's level ranks against this series' real history — the all-time percentile, then the trailing-30-year percentile, since pooling 60+ years can hide a regime shift (a few extreme years can make "normal" look lower or higher than it's felt in the modern era) — 50 is the median either way; † flags when the two readings disagree by 20 points or more, which is itself the finding.  HY / IG OAS = the extra yield investors demand to lend to junk-rated (HY) or investment-grade (IG) companies instead of the government; widening = worry, calm = confidence — credit is the market's smoke detector.  2s10s / 3m10s = long-term Treasury yield minus short-term; the slope of the yield curve.  Regime tags: calm · risk-on/confirming · risk-on/diverging · tightening · stress.
