The story

Bloomberg and CNBC both point to the same cluster of pressures — Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish tone hardening rate-hike odds to roughly 70% for this month, oil's climb reinforcing inflation worries, and (a detail specific to this cycle) reports that AI companies have raised an estimated $1.5 trillion in debt this year, which Bloomberg says is crowding out primary dealers' capacity to absorb new Treasury issuance and adding to the upward pressure on yields. Separately, Yahoo Finance and TheStreet report equities shrugged the bond story off entirely: the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq all closed higher Wednesday, "ignoring" higher oil and yields, with strength in AI-related names cited as the main driver — Dell's earnings beat (see Movers, below) is the specific name in that story.

Concept of the day · Unit 2 · Real vs. nominal decomposition · segment 4 of roughly 6

Breakevens have two pieces too — and they're the quiet half of this cycle's story

Segment 3 split the real yield into an expected-path piece and a real term-premium piece. The breakeven — the market's implied inflation rate — splits the same way: an expected-inflation piece (what investors genuinely think prices will do) and an inflation risk premium (extra compensation for the risk that inflation surprises to the upside, since bond investors are hurt more by surprise inflation than they're helped by surprise disinflation). This is why economists treat breakevens as an imperfect inflation gauge, not a market forecast read literally — a rising breakeven could mean genuinely higher expected inflation, or just a bigger risk premium, and this system can't separate the two numerically any more than it could split the real yield in segment 3. What today's data shows concretely: over the past five trading days, the breakeven has moved just 3 basis points (2.32% → 2.35%) while the real yield moved 12 basis points (2.32% → 2.44%) over the same window — the inflation-expectations side of this whole cycle has been remarkably quiet while the real-rate side has done nearly all the work. That's useful context on its own: it means the market isn't pricing a fresh inflation scare behind this month's higher yields, whatever the risk-premium piece is doing underneath.

Client translation: "The bond market's inflation forecast hasn't budged in weeks — what's actually pushing rates up is the growth-and-Fed-policy piece, not people suddenly getting worried about inflation again."
