## Concept of the day

**Unit 2 · Real vs. nominal decomposition · segment 6 of 6 (capstone) — Big headline, small move: why the size of the news and the size of the yield reaction aren't the same thing**

This unit built a toolkit for reading any yield move: split the nominal yield into a real-rate piece (TIPS real yield) and an inflation piece (breakeven), and ask which one actually moved. Friday supplies the capstone case: a payrolls report nearly three times consensus — exactly the kind of growth surprise that should hit the real-rate side hard, the way Unit 1 taught the 2-year to react to policy-path news — yet the 10-year closed up just 1 basis point. The resolution isn't that the framework failed; it's that this system's real yield and breakeven readings still carry Thursday's date, one day behind Friday's close — the pieces that would show whether the beat moved the real-rate component aren't in hand yet. A genuine limit of this system's data, not glossed over: the honest position today is "wait for tomorrow's fill," not a confident read. The broader lesson for the whole unit: a big headline doesn't guarantee a big move, and a small move doesn't mean the news didn't matter — sometimes it means the move was already priced in, and sometimes the decomposition just hasn't caught up yet. Never assume; check the pieces.

**Client translation:** "A much stronger-than-expected jobs report came out Friday, but interest rates barely budged — which usually means the bond market had already priced in a lot of that strength beforehand, not that the report didn't matter."
