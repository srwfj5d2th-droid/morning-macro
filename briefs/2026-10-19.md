## Concept of the day — Unit 6 — Positioning & sentiment vs. fundamentals, segment 3

**Priced in, or still getting worse?.** Segments 1 and 2 built the breadth toolkit: index vs. average, then rotation vs. retreat. This segment asks the harder question behind both. “Priced in” means the bad news is already reflected in the price, so the next incremental data point shouldn’t move it much. “Still worsening” means the underlying driver is still moving, so the price should keep adjusting. The test isn’t how the stock feels — it’s whether the thing actually driving it keeps changing. Here, Oracle’s five-year credit-default swaps widened to a fresh record Thursday (288bp, per Reuters), up from 260bp a week earlier; CoreWeave’s climbed above 1,000bp, a level Reuters said this system hasn’t seen reported before. If those swaps had widened once and then held flat, that would look like a one-time repricing settling into “priced in.” A cost of credit still rising week over week, alongside SMH’s ninth straight down session, is the “still worsening” pattern, not the “already priced in” one. That is exactly why CL-0042 and CL-0046 are built as live tests rather than one-time calls: a pattern that is still unfolding needs to be checked again, not just believed once.

**Client translation:** "When something keeps falling for days in a row, the question that actually matters isn't whether it feels overdone — it's whether the cost of borrowing for those companies is still climbing or has leveled off, and right now it's still climbing."
