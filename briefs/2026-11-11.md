## Concept of the day — Unit 8 — Cross-asset confirmation, segment 5

**When the hierarchy splits three ways, not two — rates, credit, and breadth can each be right about a different question.** Segments 1-4 built the hierarchy habit on a two-way split: rates against credit, or stocks against credit. Today splits three ways, and each piece is actually answering a different question. Rates and the dollar answered the policy question, cleanly: a hot CPI print pushed the 2-year yield up 17bp and December hike odds to roughly 90% — a real, data-confirmed repricing (CL-0065), not noise. Credit answered the risk question, and gave the opposite-sounding signal: HY and IG OAS both kept easing today (z +2.04 and +2.19, down from +2.46 and +2.68), a second straight session of improvement, not deterioration. Equity breadth answered a third question entirely — how widely is this being felt — and the answer is narrow: the S&P 500 fell 0.38% but the equal-weight average barely moved, down just 0.03%, meaning the selling sat almost entirely in one sector (chips, Tier 3 above) rather than across the market. None of these three actually contradicts the others once you ask what each one is measuring: more Fed hikes are coming (rates), lenders aren't newly worried about defaults (credit), and today's selling was concentrated, not systemic (breadth). The hierarchy habit isn't about picking a winner among three readings — it's matching each one to the question it's actually equipped to answer.

**Client translation:** "Rates moved today because the Fed is more likely to hike again in December — that's a different thing from markets getting nervous about defaults or a broad selloff, and today's data shows neither of those happening."

## Client lens

**“Is the Fed raising rates again?”** Today's hotter-than-expected inflation report pushed the market's odds of a December hike to around 90%, from about 72% a week ago. If that holds, it argues for things tied to short rates — new CD yields, money-market rates, credit-card APRs — staying higher for longer, even if a 30-year mortgage quote (tied more to the long end) doesn't move as much.

**“Why did Nvidia drop again?”** Today's drop was partly the broader market's reaction to the inflation report (tech led the selling) and partly the still-unresolved board investigation into the CEO's conduct — two separate things stacking on the same stock. Neither one is new information about whether companies are still buying AI chips.

**“The market barely moved today, right?”** The headline S&P 500 number (down 0.38%) undersells how narrow today's decline was — the average stock, measured without letting the giant tech names dominate, was basically flat. Two of today's three biggest movers (Charter, Take-Two) were actually up sharply on their own company news. Worth remembering before assuming a down headline day means a bad day for every account.
