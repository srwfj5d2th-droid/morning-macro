## The story

The story this morning, per quantisnow's same-day recap (“Analog Devices falls 17.3%, dragging the Nasdaq 100 down while equal-weight stocks rise”) and cnttrading's market letter (“Chip stocks give back the Huang rally as money rotates to retail”), is a reversal of Monday's Jensen-Huang-reinstatement rally. Analog Devices fell 17.37% to $312.95 even after beating Wall Street's revenue and profit estimates (Investing.com, Benzinga — adjusted EPS $4.06 vs. roughly $3.89 consensus, revenue $4.37B vs. roughly $4.31–4.32B consensus), with neither outlet tying the drop to anything specific in the print itself. Hewlett Packard Enterprise fell 16.31% to $40.19 despite beating its own earnings guidance and raising fiscal 2027 targets substantially (company release, The Motley Fool's earnings-call transcript, CryptoBriefing), while flagging that operating margin will moderate and that memory-component supply may stay tight into late calendar 2028. Micron fell 11.01% on a sharply cut BNP Paribas price target, $775 from $1,700 (247wallst), tied to memory-pricing worries ahead of Dell's own earnings; Dell itself fell 11.83% to $295.28 in the regular session, then reported record third-quarter results and a $25 billion buyback after the bell (Yahoo Finance, TradingKey) — that after-hours move won't show up in this system's numbers until tomorrow. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.48% to 23,733.54 and the S&P 500 fell a smaller 0.50% to 7,201.09, giving back a slice of Monday's 4.73% and 6.51% gains.

**This system's own numbers complicate the “market gave back the rally” read in one specific way: the giveback was narrow, not broad.** The equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP) actually rose 0.54% while the cap-weighted SPY fell 0.49% — a mirror image of Monday's gap, now running the other direction — and the Russell 2000 rose 0.57%. Credit spreads, which should widen first on a genuine risk-off day (Unit 3, Concept below), instead eased: HY OAS fell 13bp and IG OAS fell 1bp on their Monday-dated prints, both still flagged on the elevated 120-day trend but moving the calmer direction on a day the chip complex fell double digits. That combination — a severe, concentrated equity move with credit easing and breadth improving — is the live case for today's regime tag and for marking CL-0074 confirmed rather than broadening (Claims, above). Two Tier 1 readings, the 30-year Treasury and the 10-year real yield (the latter's own 23-year record reading milder once cross-checked against the Cleveland Fed's 1982-on model, History below), are still echoing levels last touched, before this run, in 2007 and 2008 respectively — worth holding in mind as today's real test arrives: Core PCE and the preliminary Q3 GDP print, both due 8:30am ET, are the first hard macro data this week that isn't one company's earnings call.

## Concept of the day — Unit 3 — Credit spreads, segment 4 reinforcement (R)

**Dispersion isn't a credit signal until OAS says so — today's chip selloff, by the numbers.** Segment 4 taught that a violent single-day move in individual stocks or a sector isn't automatically a credit event — check whether OAS, the extra yield lenders demand on top of Treasurys, actually moved before reading a big price swing as rising systemic risk. Today is a clean live case. The chip and AI-hardware complex fell hard: SMH −7.27%, Analog Devices −17.37%, Hewlett Packard Enterprise −16.31%, Micron −11.01%. If that were a genuine risk event, HY and IG OAS should have widened right alongside it. Instead both eased — HY OAS down 13bp, IG OAS down 1bp — on their Monday-dated prints.

Why this matters: dispersion (some stocks or sectors moving a lot) and systemic risk (the market's lenders getting nervous about everyone) are two different things, and only one of them should worry a diversified account. Today's test says this is dispersion, not a risk event — useful to know before assuming a scary headline about chip stocks means trouble for a client's broader bond or balanced fund.

**Client translation:** "A rough day for chip stocks isn't automatically a rough day for the credit markets that set mortgage, CD, and bond-fund rates — today, the two didn't move together, and that's informative on its own."
