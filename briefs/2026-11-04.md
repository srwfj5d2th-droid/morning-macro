## Concept of the day — Unit 7 — Financing cycles & reflexivity, segment 8 (capstone)

**Assembling the financing-cycle toolkit — a checklist for any AI-capex headline.** Seven segments now assemble into one working checklist, the same way every unit's capstone has closed before it. First: which Minsky financing type is this — does the business's own cash flow comfortably cover what's owed (hedge), cover only the interest while the principal waits to be refinanced (speculative), or does new money have to keep arriving just to service the old money (Ponzi)? Second: which lever is actually being pulled — cash flow (needs nothing from outside, but can simply run out), debt (needs the credit window to stay open), or equity (needs no lender at all, but dilutes, and gets more dilutive exactly when the stock is falling, not less — segment 6's lesson). Third: is the move about financing structure or about underlying demand, because those are different questions with different answers. Today handed the unit a clean before-and-after of all three at once. Oracle's 5.85% bounce (Movers, above) was the equity lever getting less dilutive as the stock recovered — reflexivity running the helpful direction, the exact mirror of segment 6 — a pure financing-structure story, since the raise's terms hadn't changed at all. Gartner's 13.19% drop, the same morning, was pure demand-side: a beat-and-raise quarter sold off purely on a sales-cycle comment, with no financing question involved anywhere. Same toolkit, two unrelated readings, one morning.
