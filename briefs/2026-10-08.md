## Concept of the day — Unit 5 — The dollar cycle, segment 2

**Carry and rate differentials: why money chases the currency paying more.** Yesterday's segment covered how a stronger dollar tightens conditions globally, almost mechanically, through trade and debt markets. Today's piece is about why the dollar strengthens in the first place during a stretch like this one. A big driver is simple: global investors and institutions move cash toward whichever safe, liquid currency pays the most to sit in it — this is called "carry." When U.S. short-term rates (SOFR, the 2-year Treasury, §5) sit well above what other major economies pay on their own short-term debt, holding dollars and dollar-denominated bills becomes more attractive on yield alone, independent of any view on the U.S. economy. That demand for dollars to earn the higher rate is itself what pushes the dollar's exchange value up. It's a reinforcing loop with yesterday's segment: the Fed holding rates higher for longer (confirmed hawkish by yesterday's minutes, Claims above) raises U.S. carry, which attracts capital, which strengthens the dollar (DXY at a new high today, Dashboard above), which then tightens financial conditions worldwide — a transmission channel that doesn't require a single additional rate move to keep working.

**Client translation:** "When the Fed holds rates higher than other countries, money flows here to earn that extra yield — that flow is a big part of why the dollar keeps strengthening even when the Fed doesn't lift a finger."
