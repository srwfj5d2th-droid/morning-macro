## Concept of the day

**Unit 3 · Credit spreads · segment 1 of ~10 — OAS: the extra yield lenders demand, and why it's a different signal than the level of rates**

Every bond's yield has two parts: the "risk-free" Treasury rate (today's whole subject) and a spread on top compensating a lender for default risk or liquidity risk. This system tracks that spread as OAS — option-adjusted spread — for investment-grade and high-yield credit. Treasury yields mostly reflect Fed expectations and the price of time; credit spreads reflect whether lenders are worried about being repaid at all — two separate dials, not one. Wednesday is a clean illustration: every Treasury tenor flagged as unusually high, while high-yield spreads eased and investment-grade spreads didn't move. If today's rate spike reflected real default-risk fear, credit — historically the more sensitive, forward-looking gauge — would typically move first. It didn't, which is a big part of why today's tag reads "tightening" rather than "stress."

**Client translation:** "Interest rates jumped again today — the most across-the-board move since I started sending you these — but the extra yield lenders demand from riskier, junk-rated borrowers barely budged. That's the bond market's way of saying 'borrowing got more expensive for everyone,' not 'we're worried about anyone defaulting.'"
