## Concept of the day — Unit 6 — Positioning & sentiment vs. fundamentals, segment 6

**Sympathy selling — when one company's news moves its neighbors.** Not every stock that fell today had bad news of its own. AT&T's 6.45% drop was its own earnings reaction (Movers, above), but Verizon fell 2.48% and T-Mobile fell 3% alongside it, and neither reported results today. That's sympathy selling: one company's news moves its peers even though nothing specific changed about them. The test for whether that's justified, rather than just noise, is whether the news is actually about the group, not only about the one reporter. Here it plausibly is: multiple outlets tied AT&T's subscriber miss to competition from SpaceX's Starlink Mobile service, and that competitive threat applies to Verizon's and T-Mobile's own wireless businesses just as much as AT&T's — a shared, real risk, not sentiment latching onto an unrelated name. Contrast that with Tuesday's Health Care rally (CL-0048, Claims above), where several large health-care names rose together and this system could not find any shared news tying them together at all — that one earned a claims-ledger test precisely because nothing visible connected the names, and it's already reversing after one session. The checklist either way: when a group moves on one member's news, ask what the news is actually about before assuming the whole group deserved the move.

**Client translation:** "When one phone company's earnings report sends the other phone stocks down too, that's not noise -- it usually means investors think whatever hit the one company (here, a new low-cost competitor) is a real threat to all of them, not just a one-company problem."
