## Concept of the day — Unit 1 · segment 7

**The term premium — what's left over once you subtract the expected path.** Every Treasury yield splits into two pieces: what investors expect the Fed to do with short-term rates over that bond's life (the "path"), and everything else investors demand for the uncertainty of being locked in that long — inflation surprises, supply, changing appetite for duration. That second piece is the term premium, and it's the tell for separating path-moves from premium-moves. A path move shows up mostly at the front end, where Fed expectations live (Unit 1, segment 2's Treasury-buyback example: 30Y moved, 2Y didn't — that was supply/premium, not path). A term-premium move tends to show up everywhere at once, roughly in parallel, because it isn't a view about any one Fed meeting — it's a view about the whole future. Yesterday's data point was small but textbook: the 2-year and 10-year both rose the same 2bp. That's not the Fed-expectations market repricing a specific decision; it's closer to a uniform, term-premium-flavored nudge — the kind that's easy to miss precisely because it doesn't announce itself the way a single Fed-meeting repricing does.

**Client translation:** "When your whole bond ladder's yields tick up by about the same small amount on the same day, that's usually the market asking for a little more compensation for tying up money for a long time — not a sign the Fed is about to move — so I wouldn't read next month's Fed decision into a move like yesterday's."
