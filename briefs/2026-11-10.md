## Concept of the day — Unit 8 — Cross-asset confirmation, segment 4

**Stabilizing vs. confirming — why credit easing from "worse" to "flat" isn't the same as credit agreeing with the rally.** Segment 3 showed Friday's disagreement hadn't gone away by Monday morning — stocks had bounced, credit had widened anyway. Monday adds a sharper distinction inside that same disagreement. HY OAS didn't widen again Friday; it actually eased 6bp. IG OAS didn't widen either; it just sat flat. On the surface, that looks like credit backing off its objection to the rally. It isn't, and the test for the difference is simple: did the reading cross back below the |z| 1.5 flag line, or did it just stop climbing? Both HY OAS (z +2.46) and IG OAS (z +2.68) are still flagged — still well outside their normal range, just no longer actively getting worse. That's the market-equivalent of a fever that stopped rising but hasn't broken. A client watching only the index number would see two up days and a cooling-off in the scary headlines and reasonably ask whether the all-clear has sounded; the honest answer this morning is "not yet, and here's the specific number that would tell us" — a drop back under |z| 1.5 on both series, not simply a quieter day. The discipline Unit 8 keeps teaching is the same one, applied one notch more precisely each day: don't let a signal's *direction* get mistaken for its *level*.

**Client translation:** "Credit spreads easing off their worst reading isn't the same as credit giving the rally a thumbs-up — ask whether the number actually got back to normal, not just whether it stopped getting worse."
