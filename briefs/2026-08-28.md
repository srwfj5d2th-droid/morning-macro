## Concept of the day — Unit 1 · segment 8 (capstone)

**Putting it together — reading any curve move in three questions.** Seven segments have built separate tools: naming moves (steepener/flattener), duration, convexity, and the path-versus-premium split. The capstone is one habit that uses all of them on any curve move, in order. First: *which part of the curve moved* — just the front end, just the long end, or all of it? Second: *how big, relative to duration* — the same yield-basis-point move swings a 30-year bond's price harder than a 2-year's. Third, the payoff of segment 7: *what's left after subtracting the expected path* — a front-end-only move is usually a Fed-expectations repricing; a move spread evenly across the whole curve, like today's, is usually a small term-premium touch instead. Run today's data through it: the 2-year, 10-year, and 30-year all rose the same 1bp yesterday — question one says "all of it, evenly"; question three says that pattern points to premium, not path. The honest answer is "not much happened" — exactly the right answer, and exactly the skill this system needs working a few hours before a Fed chair speaks.

**Client translation:** "There's a repeatable way to tell whether a Treasury-yield move is the market repricing what the Fed does next or just investors wanting a little more compensation for lending long-term — and today's move, small and even across every maturity, is the second kind, not a signal to change anything."
