The story

No fresh weekend catalyst — Friday’s story just kept running. A search for weekend developments (geopolitics, futures, policy announcements) turned up nothing dated Saturday or Sunday that changed the picture; the Fed’s pre-meeting quiet period (Oct 17–29, Chicago Fed calendar) started Saturday, so no officials have spoken since Chair Warsh’s Thursday-night remarks in Bangkok. CNN’s Friday close led with “Nasdaq sinks more than 2% as investors raise questions about AI debt,” reporting the Nasdaq Composite’s worst week since June; Reuters tied the session to a hotter-than-expected inflation print feeding rate-hike bets, and quoted D.A. Davidson’s Gil Luria on whether CoreWeave “can earn enough to repay its borrowing.” CNBC had reported Wednesday that CoreWeave’s bonds were trading near 15% yields on the same AI-borrowing worry; Reuters, citing LSEG, put CoreWeave’s five-year credit-default swaps (insurance against a default) above 1,000bp for the first time, and Oracle’s five-year swaps at a record 288bp, up from 260bp a week earlier.

What the data shows: the selloff widened, not just in one or two names. The S&P 500 fell 1.09% Friday to 7,659.44, 2.04% off its Oct 6 record; the Nasdaq fell 2.14%; the Russell 2000 fell 1.66%. 51 S&P names and sector funds qualified for this system’s movers screen, and the move was lopsided: every one of the dozen-plus biggest decliners sat in the same complex — chipmakers, storage, optical networking, and high-beta software (Movers, below) — while the gainers were almost entirely energy and defense names. The chip fund SMH fell 3.41%, a ninth straight daily decline, down 16.46% from its Jun 22 six-month high per this system’s own tracking. Bonds rallied into the risk-off tape: the 10-year fell 1bp to 5.09% and the 2-year fell 3bp to 4.74%, a mild bull steepener (2s10s widened 2bp to 35bp) — the mirror image of Thursday’s bull flattener. The 10-year has now sat at or above 5.09% since Sep 23; before this run, it was last there in July 2007 (Today in history, below).

Oil jumped and no outlet this system found explained why. WTI rose 3.51% to $101.41, its biggest one-day move since Oct 8, while energy stocks (XLE) still gained 2.52% — unlike Thursday, when oil rose and energy stocks fell for reasons no outlet explained, Friday’s energy-sector move and the oil move agree. Why this matters: a sustained move above $100 WTI shows up at the pump before it shows up anywhere else in a client’s budget, and this system flags it as a watch item, not a conclusion, until a named source explains the move.

The press framing, read plainly. CNN’s and Reuters’ own framing — AI-infrastructure borrowing costs are rising and investors are starting to ask whether the revenue is there to cover them — is their reading, not a conclusion this system can reach from price data alone. What the state file can say: credit spreads for junk-rated borrowers in aggregate (HY OAS) have stayed above both Oct 1’s level and this run’s confirm line for CL-0046 without snapping back, and two single-name credit-default-swap spreads widened further. That is consistent with the press framing; it is not, by itself, proof of it.

Concept of the day · Unit 6 — Positioning & sentiment vs. fundamentals, segment 3

Priced in, or still getting worse?

Segments 1 and 2 built the breadth toolkit: index vs. average, then rotation vs. retreat. This segment asks the harder question behind both. “Priced in” means the bad news is already reflected in the price, so the next incremental data point shouldn’t move it much. “Still worsening” means the underlying driver is still moving, so the price should keep adjusting. The test isn’t how the stock feels — it’s whether the thing actually driving it keeps changing. Here, Oracle’s five-year credit-default swaps widened to a fresh record Thursday (288bp, per Reuters), up from 260bp a week earlier; CoreWeave’s climbed above 1,000bp, a level Reuters said this system hasn’t seen reported before. If those swaps had widened once and then held flat, that would look like a one-time repricing settling into “priced in.” A cost of credit still rising week over week, alongside SMH’s ninth straight down session, is the “still worsening” pattern, not the “already priced in” one. That is exactly why CL-0042 and CL-0046 are built as live tests rather than one-time calls: a pattern that is still unfolding needs to be checked again, not just believed once.

Client translation: When something keeps falling for days in a row, the question that actually matters isn't whether it feels overdone — it's whether the cost of borrowing for those companies is still climbing or has leveled off, and right now it's still climbing.

Reading key
bp (basis point) = 1/100th of a percent — "+4bp" on a yield means it rose 0.04%.  z(120d) = how unusual today's level is versus the past ~6 months of trading days; 0 is typical, and anything beyond ±1.5 gets a ⚑ flag — far enough outside its recent normal range to deserve attention.  %ile all/30y = where today's level ranks against this series' real history — the all-time percentile, then the trailing-30-year percentile, since pooling 60+ years can hide a regime shift (a few extreme years can make "normal" look lower or higher than it's felt in the modern era) — 50 is the median either way; † flags when the two readings disagree by 20 points or more, which is itself the finding.  HY / IG OAS = the extra yield investors demand to lend to junk-rated (HY) or investment-grade (IG) companies instead of the government; widening = worry, calm = confidence — credit is the market's smoke detector.  2s10s / 3m10s = long-term Treasury yield minus short-term; the slope of the yield curve.  Regime tags: calm · risk-on/confirming · risk-on/diverging · tightening · stress.
