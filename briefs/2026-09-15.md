## Concept of the day — Unit 3, Credit spreads, segment 4 of ~10

**A stock swinging 13% in a day isn't a credit signal either — the false-positive mode is dispersion, not just a rate spike.** Friday's segment used OAS to tell a Fed-driven rate spike apart from a "something's breaking" spike. Today's dashboard hands a related test: individual stocks moving double digits in a session — CrowdStrike up almost 14%, Corning down almost 14%, the same morning — can look like the dispersion that precedes a real credit event. It usually isn't, and the same tool tells you why: check whether OAS actually moved. It didn't today — HY eased, IG was flat — strong evidence this is a sector-level reassessment (capital rotating between AI-hardware suppliers and cybersecurity firms), not the market pricing a wave of defaults or a liquidity squeeze. A genuine credit event almost always shows up as spreads widening across a broad swath of borrowers, not just certain stock prices falling.

**Client translation:** "Seeing a stock — or even a whole group of stocks — swing double digits in a day feels alarming, but it's not the same signal as a credit problem; the tell is still whether corporate borrowing costs are moving, and today they aren't."
