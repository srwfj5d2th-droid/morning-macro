## Concept of the day — Unit 5 — The dollar cycle, segment 4

**The dollar moves in long waves: read today's level against the whole cycle.** The first three segments covered how a strong dollar tightens conditions, why carry pulls money toward higher U.S. rates, and how that tightening spreads abroad. Today's piece is about time scale. The dollar doesn't swing day to day the way stocks do; it moves in waves that last years. So two yardsticks answer two different questions. The 120-day z-score (+2.04 on Friday) says the dollar is strong compared with the past six months. The long record says where that sits in the cycle. Since 1971, DXY (the dollar against a basket of major currencies) has ranged from 71.33, its 2000s low, to 164.72, its 1980s high. Friday's 102.21 is the 72nd percentile of that whole history and the 81st of the last 30 years. By decade, the dollar was at or above Friday's level on 57.7% of days in the 1980s, but on only 1.6% of days in the 1990s and 0.6% in the 2010s. Its latest peak, 114.11 in September 2022, was well above today. Put together: strong by recent memory, inside the long range. That matters for clients with international funds, since a dollar this strong shrinks what foreign holdings are worth in dollars, and the long record says it has gone both higher and much lower.

**Client translation:** "The dollar moves in long waves: it's strong right now for anyone who remembers the 2010s, but ordinary for anyone who remembers the '80s, and over years it can swing a long way in either direction."
