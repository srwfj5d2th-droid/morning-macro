## Concept of the day — Unit 8 — Cross-asset confirmation, segment 6

**A fourth vote joins the hierarchy — the dollar tends to side with rates, not credit, because both price off the same real-rate input.** Segment 5 showed the hierarchy splitting three ways — rates, credit, and breadth each answering a different question. This week adds a fourth voice: the dollar. DXY's climb to this run's largest flag (z +2.44, Chart of the day, above) isn't really a separate opinion from the front-end-led rate rise (Dashboard, above) — both are priced off the same thing, how much the Fed is expected to keep rates elevated, which is why they tend to move together rather than disagree. That's different from credit's job: credit prices default and liquidity risk, a genuinely separate question from the rate path, which is exactly why HY OAS eased this week (z +1.38, cleared its flag) even as rates and the dollar both climbed — not a contradiction, just two different gauges answering two different questions, with the dollar siding with rates rather than with credit because of what it's actually built to measure.

The practical test: when the dollar and rates move together, that's not independent confirmation the way credit agreeing with equities would be — it's closer to the same signal measured twice. Independent confirmation has to come from a gauge pricing a genuinely different risk, which is why credit (and, this week, breadth — RSP's +0.20% day even as the S&P fell) still carry more weight in the hierarchy than a second rates-linked series would.

**Client translation:** "The dollar getting stronger and interest rates rising together this week aren't two separate warning signs — they're basically the same signal (the market expecting the Fed to stay higher for longer) showing up in two places, which is different from credit markets actually getting nervous about defaults, and they haven't."
