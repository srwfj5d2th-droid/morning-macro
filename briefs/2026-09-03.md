## Concept of the day

**Unit 2 · Real vs. nominal decomposition · segment 4 of roughly 6 — Breakevens have two pieces too — and they're the quiet half of this cycle's story**

Segment 3 split the real yield into an expected-path piece and a real term-premium piece. The breakeven splits the same way: an **expected-inflation** piece (what investors genuinely think prices will do) and an **inflation risk premium** (extra compensation for the risk that inflation surprises to the upside). This is why breakevens are an imperfect inflation gauge, not a market forecast read literally — this system can't separate the two pieces numerically any more than it could split the real yield in segment 3. What today's data shows concretely: over the past five trading days, the breakeven moved just 3bp (2.32%→2.35%) while the real yield moved 12bp (2.32%→2.44%) over the same window — the inflation-expectations side of this cycle has been remarkably quiet while the real-rate side has done nearly all the work.

**Client translation:** "The bond market's inflation forecast hasn't budged in weeks — what's actually pushing rates up is the growth-and-Fed-policy piece, not people suddenly getting worried about inflation again."
