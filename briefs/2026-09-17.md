## The story

Reporting this morning centers on the Fed. CNBC, Yahoo Finance, Fox Business, and CNN Business all confirm the FOMC voted 12-0 Wednesday to raise the federal funds target range a quarter point to 3.75–4.00% — the first hike since July 2023 — a move futures markets had priced at roughly 93% odds heading into the decision (see yesterday's brief). CNBC's own framing is the one that matters most: "the real surprise... was not the 25 basis point increase. It was the message behind it" — the updated dot plot showing 16 of 18 officials expecting at least one more hike this year, with the median projection holding near 4.1% through 2027, a "higher for longer" signal that CNBC reports left both stocks and bonds lower into the close as Chair Warsh's press conference read as more hawkish on inflation than markets wanted. Two smaller threads ran underneath: J.B. Hunt fell 13.3% — per The Motley Fool, CNBC, and Seeking Alpha, its worst trading day in the four decades since its 1983 IPO — after warning Q3 earnings would drop 5–10% on $25 million of added driver costs and $10 million-plus of fuel costs, with AAA pegging Wednesday's national diesel average at an all-time high of $6.31/gallon; the warning dragged the broader trucking sector down with it (Seeking Alpha). And oil pulled back: TradingKey and apacnewsnetwork report WTI eased after a surprise 7.14-million-barrel U.S. inventory build (versus a forecast 1.8-million-barrel draw) and fresh OPEC/IEA demand-growth downgrades, dragging the energy sector down with it even as the Middle East supply-risk story this system has tracked since August (CL-0007, CL-0017) remains technically intact. A brighter counter-thread: Lumentum jumped roughly 9% (Deutsche Bank, Evercore ISI, StocksToTrade) on a management guidance raise and a wave of bullish AI-optics analyst coverage, with Coherent rallying alongside it on Ciena's own optimistic long-term targets — a guidance-driven bounce that partly reverses Monday's AI-caution scare (CL-0015, above).

**Does the state file confirm the press's framing?** On rates, cleanly and immediately: every Treasury tenor this system tracks stays flagged, the 2-year posted the single largest one-day move on the board (+7bp to 4.74%, z +3.12) while the 10-year barely budged (+1bp, z +2.82) — the front end repricing the policy path while the long end, which was already close to fairly priced, moved far less. That asymmetry shows up directly in the curve: 2s10s (the 2-year-to-10-year gap) narrowed 6bp to 27bp and is itself now flagged (z −2.28) — a textbook post-hike flattener, not a broad risk-off move. Equities complicate the loudest version of the press's "market sell-off" framing: the S&P 500 fell a modest 0.45% and the Nasdaq-100 was essentially flat (−0.01%) — a real but unremarkable one-day move (z +0.54, unflagged), a long way from a rout. What the state file can't yet confirm or complicate is the question that actually matters most for whether "higher for longer" is a benign repricing or a real tightening-of-financial-conditions event: credit. Both HY and IG OAS are still dated Tuesday 09-15, a full session behind the decision itself (§5's 1-day publication lag) — tomorrow's fill is the live test, and it anchors both today's new claim (CL-0018) and today's curriculum segment (below).
