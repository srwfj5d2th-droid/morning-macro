## Concept of the day

**Unit 2 · Real vs. nominal decomposition · segment 2 of roughly 6 — Reading which piece moved: Friday's hawkish repricing, decomposed**

Yesterday's segment established the identity: nominal 10-year yield ≈ real yield (TIPS) + breakeven inflation. Today, put it to work on an actual event. Friday's hawkish Jackson Hole speech pushed the nominal 10-year yield up 6bp, from 4.67% Thursday to 4.73% Friday. Split it: the real yield rose 8bp (2.34% → 2.42%); the breakeven actually *fell* 2bp (2.33% → 2.31%). Add the two moves together (+8bp and −2bp) and you get +6bp, matching the nominal move to the basis point. That's the tell: Friday's yield spike was almost entirely a real-yield story, not an inflation story — the market got more convinced the Fed will keep policy tight for longer, not more worried about inflation returning. A move driven by breakevens instead would point to a very different, inflation-scare narrative.

**Client translation:** "When yields jump the way they did Friday, the first question I ask is whether it's the inflation piece or the real, after-inflation piece that moved — Friday it was almost entirely the real piece, which tells us this was a 'the Fed is staying tougher for longer' move, not a fresh inflation scare."
