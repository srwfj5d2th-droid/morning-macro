## Concept of the day — Unit 2 · segment 1 of ~6

**Splitting a Treasury yield into two prices: growth-and-inflation, and time.** Unit 1 treated the 10-year Treasury yield as one number. It's actually two, added together. The real yield (measured by TIPS) is roughly what investors demand to lend money for ten years after stripping out inflation. The breakeven is the market's implied average inflation rate over that period. Add them together and you get (approximately) the plain nominal yield everyone quotes — not exact (a small liquidity sliver separates the two bond types), but close enough to be useful. Thursday's data (08-27, the most recent date with both figures published) makes the arithmetic almost exact: TIPS real yield 2.34% + breakeven 2.33% = 4.67%, that same day's nominal 10-year yield to the basis point. The payoff: when the nominal yield moves, this decomposition tells you whether markets are repricing growth expectations, inflation expectations, or both.

**Client translation:** "When we say the 10-year Treasury yield, that number is really two things stacked together — what investors want to be paid for expected inflation, and what they want for tying up money for ten years — and separating them tells us whether a rate move is really an inflation story or a growth story."
